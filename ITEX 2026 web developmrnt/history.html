The History of Sigiriya

Discover the remarkable story behind Sri Lanka's ancient Lion Rock.

A Fortress Above the Clouds

Sigiriya is one of Sri Lanka's most remarkable historical and archaeological sites. Rising approximately 180 metres above the surrounding plains, the massive rock became an important centre of power during the fifth century CE. Its most famous period began during the reign of King Kashyapa I, who ruled Sri Lanka from approximately 477 to 495 CE.

According to historical traditions, Kashyapa selected Sigiriya as the location for his royal residence and constructed a fortified palace complex on and around the rock. The site was carefully designed with impressive gardens, pools, walls and elaborate water-management systems. Visitors approaching the summit passed through a monumental entrance associated with a lion, giving the fortress its famous name, "Lion Rock."

Sigiriya is also renowned for its ancient paintings and architectural achievements. The famous frescoes depict graceful female figures and demonstrate the artistic skill of the period. The Mirror Wall, once highly polished, contains inscriptions and graffiti left by visitors centuries ago. These writings provide valuable evidence about how people viewed and visited Sigiriya after its royal period.

Following Kashyapa's death, Sigiriya gradually lost its role as a royal capital and became associated with Buddhist monastic life. Monks occupied the area for centuries before the site was eventually abandoned. In modern times, archaeological research brought renewed attention to Sigiriya, revealing the complexity of its architecture, art and engineering. Today, Sigiriya is recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site and remains one of Sri Lanka's most important cultural landmarks.
